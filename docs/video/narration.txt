## 1 af_bella
Every app you build has to answer two questions.

Who is this person?

...

And what may they do here?

Without a shared answer, every app keeps its own list of users and passwords.

Identity is the one place that answers for the whole ecosystem.

People sign in once. Each app lists its roles. Administrators decide who holds them.

In this video we will set it up, connect an app, and see what the app receives.

## 2 af_bella

## 6 af_bella
Now the sign-in flow, the part people see.

The app sends the browser to Identity.

Grace signs in there, on Identity's own page. So the app never sees her password.

[pause 1.5]

Identity sends her back with a one-time code. The app swaps it for tokens, using its secret and a random check value called PKCE.

...

Notes now knows her name and her roles. She is a reader, so the page says read only.

[pause 1.0]

Now she signs out. That ends her session at Identity too.

Now Ada signs in.

[pause 4.0]

Ada holds the editor role, so she can edit notes.

...

Identity also has a launcher page. It lists every app a person may open, with their roles.

## 7 af_bella
Back on the Notes page, this box shows what the app received. These are the claims of Ada's ID token.

The app did not call Identity to ask who she is. The token is signed, and the app checks that signature.

It checks the issuer, which is Identity.

And the audience, which is this app and no other.

The roles inside are only the ones for Notes.

...

The signature check uses Identity's public keys. The app finds them through the discovery document.

[pause 2.0]

That document lists every address an app needs. The keys are published here.

[pause 2.0]

So connecting an app takes just three settings. Where Identity is. The client id. And the secret.

## 8 am_michael
Now, services. They need to sign in too, with no person involved.

We register a service, a background worker, and say exactly what it may ask for. The scope notes.write, for the notes app only.

[pause 3.5]

It gets its own secret. We copy it.

[pause 1.0]

Now the worker asks for a token.

[pause 2.5]

The subject is the worker, marked as a service. The audience is notes. The scope is notes.write.

...

Now it asks for a scope it was never given.

[pause 1.5]

Identity refuses: invalid scope.

This replaces copying one shared password around. Tokens last ten minutes.

## 9 am_michael
Day to day, three things matter.

First, people. We disable Alan.

[pause 1.0]

Now he cannot sign in. His long-lived sessions end at once, and tokens he already holds expire within ten minutes.

[pause 3.0]

Second, the signing keys. Here is the active one. We rotate it.

[pause 2.0]

The new key is active. The old one is retiring, and stays published for twenty-four hours. Nothing breaks mid-flight.
